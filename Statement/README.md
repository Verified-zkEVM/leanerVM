# Statement

What the repository proves, in the files a reviewer reads: definitions and end statements only,
one Lake package (`statement`) of fifteen files, `Statement/<Name>.lean` (Lean modules
`Statement.<Name>`), the leanISA guest program's generated bytecode in [`Data/`](Data/), and, for
speed only, proved `@[csimp]` lemmas in [`Fast/`](Fast/), which a reviewer need not read, and the
honest prover's definitions in [`Prover/`](Prover/), which a reviewer checks only for being a
genuine prover (below). It imports Mathlib, VCVio and CompPoly and nothing else (CI checks it);
every other project of the repository is proof and imports it.

**The model.** Every end statement with an adversary is in the random-permutation model: one
uniformly random permutation `π` of the 1600-bit Keccak state, which every party queries forward
(`π x`) and inverse (`π⁻¹ y`), Keccak-256 the sponge over `π` and Keccak-f[1600] `π` itself. That
the deployed Keccak-f[1600], a fixed permutation, behaves as a random one is the assumption,
even to programs that compute it: in the model the `KECCAK` instruction a proved program executes
computes the deployed Keccak-f[1600], independent of the transcript's `π`, which excludes by
construction programs that evaluate their own proof's Fiat-Shamir duplex (the setting of the known
attacks on Fiat-Shamir for GKR-based systems, Khovratovich, Rothblum and Soukhanov, 2025). The
adversary is classical, its code fixed before `π` is sampled; quantum adversaries are not covered.
`Statement/` contains no random oracle: the random-oracle model and the sponge's
indifferentiability, through which the proofs pass, are proof steps, and their definitions
(the two-oracle and duplex simulators, Wagner's reductions and his random-oracle terms) are in
`common` and `lean_da`. LeanDA's code binding is conditional on the argument's knowledge
soundness in the random-permutation model, the only term of its bound that depends on the
argument; an argument whose relation uses the deployed Keccak-f[1600] instead of `π` (leanVM
proving the guest program's DA check) cannot meet that hypothesis in this model.

**The sources.** The leanVM definitions are transcribed from leanVM at
`c445e3d51a5a85031df47240595698f3b24897bf` (branch `main`, [`../leanvm-rev`](../leanvm-rev)): each
cites its source (`verifier.py:N`, `*.rs:N`, `doc/leanvm` sections). LeanDA's security framework is
Benedikt Wagner's ([b-wagn/fv-pq-da](https://github.com/b-wagn/fv-pq-da), Apache-2.0, see
[`../lean_da/NOTICE`](../lean_da/NOTICE)).

**What is trusted.** That the deployed Keccak-f[1600] behaves as a random permutation; that no one
finds a Keccak-256 collision (the guest program's statements); that the cost machine's prices are
honest (among them a `GF(2^64)` product at `256`, on the basis of a hardware carry-less multiply);
and that the leanVM definitions transcribe their sources. Accepted trade-offs: the statement does
not name the extractor or decoder program (only their sizes and budgets); the decoder computes the
witness from the execution; the extracted execution need not be the committed one; the decoder's
budget has no cap on steps, grows as `steps·log steps` below `2^61` steps (`steps·log² steps` as
they grow), and its constant (`2^42.0` to `2^44.2` at the points below, the address tables at the
execution's `κ` and the program's `ℓ` included) prices the witness at the bounds a valid execution's
asserts and its children's accepted proofs give (`16` children, each with `65535` claims of each
scheme and a cursor of `8192` words, `65535` declared claims of each scheme, `1024` rows of the DA
matrix), not at the run's, so with fewer children and claims, at `κ ≤ 28` and well below `2^21`
steps, the budget is several times the compiled decoder's price (at `κ = 32` the address tables,
priced at the run's `κ`, are most of it); that
a valid execution of the guest program has fewer than `2^51` steps is argued in prose, not proved in
Lean; literal tables count toward size; the size bounds limit programs written like the compiled
ones (a decoder written differently could pack the guest's tables tighter, about `1600` signatures'
room); the extractor's budget is `2^95 + O(N·log³N)` for `N` queries.

## Reading order

The entry point is [`Theorems.lean`](Theorems.lean) (item 14): skim it first, for the list of end
theorems, then read the files in this order.

1. [`Keccak.lean`](Keccak.lean): Keccak-f[1600] as leanVM computes it (`keccakF1600`, on
   `KeccakState`, `25` lanes of `64` bits: the one state type of the statement) and Keccak-256
   over any permutation of the state in any monad (`KeccakHash.keccak256With`), the deployed one
   over Keccak-f[1600] (`keccak256`). No hash is an oracle or a parameter of its own: every party
   hashes with Keccak-256 over the permutation it is given (`keccak256With`), `π` in the model.
2. [`RandomPermutation.lean`](RandomPermutation.lean): the random-permutation model
   (`permutationOracleSpec`: `π` and `π⁻¹`; `lazyRandomPermutation`, the lazily sampled uniform
   permutation); a cost bounded on every path (`QueryCostAtMost`); the oracles of the model
   (`RandomPermutationOracles`: coins, `π` and `π⁻¹` on `KeccakState`, nothing else;
   `queryPermutation`, a call of `π`), a game's cost in calls of `π` and `π⁻¹`
   (`permutationCallCost`), its run against the lazy permutation (`runWithRandomPermutation`) and a
   computation's queries recorded (`loggedOracles`, its transcript `OracleQueryLog`).
3. [`SufCma.lean`](SufCma.lean): the SUF-CMA game of a hash-based signature scheme over `π`
   (`SufCma.forgeryGame`, `forgeryExperiment`) and its security notion
   (`SufCma.StronglyUnforgeable`).
4. [`HashSignatures.lean`](HashSignatures.lean): what leanXMSS and leanSPHINCS share (namespace
   `HashSig`): the tweakable hash's types, bytes and address (`crates/signatures/src/tweak.rs`),
   the hash `H`, Keccak-256 over a permutation `π` (`Concrete.keccakHash π`), and a hash chain, a
   Merkle path's fold and a Merkle tree's node (`walkChain`, `foldPath`, `buildTree`), which every
   chain, fold and tree of both schemes instantiates.
5. [`LeanXmss.lean`](LeanXmss.lean): the leanXMSS scheme over a permutation `π`, on the shared
   part, and `LeanXmss.Rpm.LeanXmssStronglyUnforgeable` (`StronglyUnforgeable` at 127 bits, `Q`
   counting every permutation call of the game, key generation and the honest signer's worst-case
   grinding included: about `2^-62` at a full lifetime).
6. [`LeanSphincs.lean`](LeanSphincs.lean): the leanSPHINCS scheme (on the shared part, exported
   into `Sphincs`; one tree, its signature the randomizer, the forest opening, the WOTS+C counter
   and chain values and the path) with the deterministic signer, the proved lifetimes, and
   `Sphincs.Lifetimes.LeanSphincsStronglyUnforgeable` (`StronglyUnforgeable` at 127 bits at each
   lifetime, `Q` counting the honest signer's worst-case grinding too).
7. [`Field.lean`](Field.lean): `K = GF(2^64)` (CompPoly's `BF64`), `E = GF(2^192)`, the generator
   `g`; Keccak lanes, bytes and digests as field elements.
8. [`LeanDa.lean`](LeanDa.lean): LeanDA (fields, Reed–Solomon rows, the Merkle commitment, the
   membership test), the parts of Benedikt Wagner's framework the end statements name (the
   consistency of opened cells), the relation of LeanDA's argument (the root's challenges drawn by
   the deployed duplex over a permutation), arguments that query the model's oracles, the commitment
   and the challenges' duplex over a permutation, LeanDA's games in the random-permutation model,
   where the adversary, the argument (`OracleArgument`, its extractor reading the adversary's
   transcript) and the verifier query the same oracles (`RandomPermutationOracles`, hashing with
   Keccak-256 over `π`, `keccakDigestBytes queryPermutation`; position binding, code binding, the
   argument's knowledge soundness `knowledgeSoundnessGame`, completeness for every permutation `π`),
   and `LeanDa.PerfectlyCompleteRpm`, `LeanDa.PositionBindingRpm`, `LeanDa.CodeBindingRpm`. Code
   binding is conditional: if the argument is knowledge sound in the random-permutation model with
   error `(q + c) · 2^-k`, LeanDA is code binding with error
   `(Q + c + 2^22) · 2^-k + (Q + 2^22) · 2^-188 + (Q + 2^22)² · 2^-247` for every `Q` and `r ≤ 1024`
   rows (a straight-line proof on Wagner's framework); the last term passes 1 at about
   `Q = 2^123.5`. `CodeBindingRpm`'s docstring says which arguments satisfy the hypothesis.
9. [`LeanIsa.lean`](LeanIsa.lean): leanISA (memory, the six instructions, programs, one step, `run`,
   `ValidExecution`: a run from the initial state `(pcInitial, fpInitial)` that halts in the final
   state `(pcFinal, fpFinal)`, the instruction counts `Execution.count`, the bytecode encoding, the
   bytecode table the verifier reads, `bytecodeWords`, and the public input's digest,
   `publicDigest`, with its inverse, `PublicInput.ofDigest`), and a program assembled from compiled
   functions (`CompiledFn`, `assemble`).
10. [`LeanVmVerifier.lean`](LeanVmVerifier.lean): the verifier, `python-verifier/verifier.py`
   transcribed section by section, over any permutation in any monad, Keccak-256 the sponge over it
   (`verifyWith`), with the WHIR constants it reads, the Keccak GKR reduction, the Fiat-Shamir
   duplex over Keccak-f[1600], the deferring verifier an aggregate runs on its children, and the
   sizes a stream announces (`announced`). Where the transcription's form differs from the pin's
   without changing what it computes, its docstring gives the pin's lines and why the two agree.
11. [`LeanVm.lean`](LeanVm.lean): a statement (a program and a public input), the verifier on it
   over a permutation (`verifier F`), the honest prover's type and **the honest prover**, `prover`
   (`LeanVM.Prover.prove` of [`Prover/`](Prover/)), the committed size; the random-permutation
   experiment (`soundnessExperiment`, one game: an adversary with private coins,
   `RandomPermutationAdversary`, its queries to `π` and `π⁻¹` counted by `permutationCallCost` and
   recorded, then the verifier over `π`), a straight-line extractor's type (`StraightLineExtractor`:
   from the statement, the transcript and the proof, an execution or none), the transcript's
   permutation queries (`permutationQueries`), **`LeanVM.KnowledgeSoundness extractor`** (for a
   given extractor; it asks for some valid execution, not necessarily the one the proof commits to),
   **`LeanVM.Completeness`**.
12. [`CostMachine.lean`](CostMachine.lean): the cost machine (`MachineValue`, `PrimitiveOperation`,
    `Term`, `MachineProgram`, `evaluateTerm`, `run`, `outputWithin`, a program's `size`: a
    first-order functional language on words, natural numbers and arrays, each step priced at least
    `1` and, but for a `case`'s fields and a closure's captured values, at least the words it
    writes, its operations priced by their algorithms), the encoding of the extractor's input and
    output (`encodeExtractorInput`, `decodeExecution`, a linear parse), the budget
    (`extractorBudget`, read off the shape a proof announces: `announced`, `LeanVmVerifier.lean`)
    and **`LeanVM.extractorOf P`**: the extractor a program `P` computes, run within the budget, its
    output parsed; `Statement.Theorems` takes `P` with its size at most `extractorSizeBound` and
    does not name it (`lean_vm` names its knowledge extractor, compiled). That the machine's prices
    are honest is trusted. The size bound is `14400`: the compiled extractor's `13813` and a slack
    of about one leanXMSS signature's encoding, so the compiled extractor could carry no useful
    table of witnesses beside its code; the budget (`extractorBudgetFor`, wherever the proof
    announces a shape) is a short formula in the number `N` of queries, `2^95 + (N + 2^30)·(D +
    2)²·(2^15 + 2D)` with `D = log₂(N + 2) + 1`: about `2^95` for `N ≤ 2^64` (the list decoder,
    about `2^94.07` at the worst shape) and `2^157.08` at `N = 2^128` (the transcript's index),
    where the soundness bound is already trivial; `lean_vm` proves the compiled extractor's price
    below it at every shape the replay admits.
13. [`LeanVmGuest.lean`](LeanVmGuest.lean): the leanISA guest program's specification (aggregation):
    DA claims and their digest, a node's statement, its digest (Keccak-256 of a preimage), witness
    and relation (`NodeRelation`: leanXMSS and leanSPHINCS claims, a DA digest, children verified by
    the deferring verifier, the batching of their deferred claims), a decoding (`Decoded`: a seed, a
    statement, a witness) and what it explains of a public input (`ExplainsInput`), the candidate
    collisions of two statements' digests and **`LeanVM.Node.DigestBinds`** (a digest binds the seed
    and a statement of a given shape, up to a Keccak-256 collision), the deployed leanISA guest
    program (`LeanVM.Guest.guest`, from [`Data/`](Data/)), its deployed limits (`Limits`, and of
    each child `AcceptableChild`, its zero-challenge refusal `Prog.refusingZeroChallenge`),
    **`LeanVM.Guest.Budget.GuestCompleteCovered`** (the nodes `CoveredNode` lists), the honest proof
    as a child (`verifierRefusingZero`, `honestProofWithDeployedKeccak`, **`GuestChildComplete`**);
    and the recursion step: the batching of the children's claims as a verifier program
    (`boundedBatchingProgram`, run from the batching's seed by `runBatching`), an adversary's proof
    run through it over `π` in the random-permutation model (one game), the event that it accepts
    children with a false claim (`SomeChildClaimFalse`), the program's query bound
    (`batchingQueryBound`), and **`LeanVM.Guest.Batch.BatchingSound`**: the batching run outside the
    circuit with Keccak-f[1600] = `π`, which does not compose with the guest program's in-circuit
    statements (their `KECCAK` is the deployed Keccak-f[1600]), so soundness across recursion levels
    does not follow from it.
14. [`Theorems.lean`](Theorems.lean): every end theorem, as the fields of `Statement.Theorems`;
    first, the guest decoder as a cost-machine program `D`: the encodings of a decoding (a seed, a
    statement and a witness, `encodeValue`), `D`'s input (`encodeDecoderInput`: the guest program
    and the execution), its size bound (`guestDecoderSizeBound`) and budget (`guestDecoderBudget`),
    and **`LeanVM.Node.GuestKnowledge guest D`** (on every valid
    execution, `D`, run within its budget, outputs the encoding of a decoding that explains the
    public input, `ExplainsInput`; no parse). `D` may compute the witness from the execution by any
    algorithm (the proofs' decoder reads it off the memory), as the extractor program may compute an
    execution by any algorithm. The two programs have the same type, so every use of
    `Statement.Theorems` names them (`(extractorProgram := P) (decoderProgram := D)`). The decoder's
    size bound, budget and their limits: see [The guest decoder's size bound and
    budget](#the-guest-decoders-size-bound-and-budget) and **What is trusted**.
15. [`Corollaries.lean`](Corollaries.lean), optional for review: statements that follow from
    `Theorems` alone, in their composed form (`lean_vm` proves `Corollaries` from `Theorems` at the
    same two programs): what an accepted proof of the leanISA guest program establishes
    (`LeanVM.Node.AggregateSound`, with `ExplainsStatement`: `KnowledgeSoundness`, `GuestKnowledge`
    and `DigestBinds` composed) and the completeness on recursion nodes and leaves
    (`GuestCompleteRecursion`, `GuestCompleteLeaf`, the first two families of `CoveredNode`).

[`Data/`](Data/) is the guest program's compiled bytecode, generated from leanVM's sources by
`lean_vm/scripts/guest-export` (do not edit; `guest-export --check` fails when it is stale, as CI
runs it). It needs no review beyond that check.

[`Fast/`](Fast/) makes the compiled definitions fast and changes nothing they mean: `@[csimp]`
lemmas, each proved, that compile `GF(2^64)` products on machine words (`FieldWords`), the
degree-three extension's product unrolled (`CubicExt`), Keccak-f[1600] with its lanes written out
and Keccak-256 over any permutation with its blocks read from arrays (`Keccak`), a digest's bytes
read as a number by a fold (`SufCma`), and the signature schemes' verifiers with a constant-depth
lookup (`Schemes`). A `@[csimp]` lemma acts where a definition is compiled, so they are here,
imported by `Field.lean`, `HashSignatures.lean`, `LeanDa.lean` and `LeanVmGuest.lean`, for the
statement's own compiled definitions (the verifier, the prover, the signature schemes, the guest
program's specification, LeanDA) and every module after them. They need no review: a `@[csimp]`
lemma is an equation the kernel checks.

[`Prover/`](Prover/) is leanVM's honest prover as an executable program,
`LeanVM.Prover.prove F prog input t ρ fuel` (`Prove.lean`), over a permutation `F` in any monad,
Keccak-256 the sponge over `F`: over the model's `π` it is the prover `LeanVM.prover` that
`Completeness` and `GuestChildComplete` name, and over Keccak-f[1600] in `Id` it is
`lake exe leanvm-prove`, whose proofs the Rust and Lean verifiers accept (`lean_vm/README.md`). It
is definitions only: the witness from the trace (`Witness.lean`, `Columns.lean`: one run of the
program; no proof, `none`, unless the trace is a valid execution within `2^28` committed cells), a
script of the prover's moves (`Script.lean`) for each phase of the verifier (`Ntt.lean`, `Bus.lean`,
`Gkr.lean`, `Table.lean`, `Public.lean`, `Keccak*.lean`, `Ring.lean`, `Whir.lean`; `Mle.lean`,
`Sumcheck.lean`, `Code.lean` their helpers), and its run against the verifier's own program
(`Run.lean`: the challenges squeezed from the sponge, each Merkle tree built once, the proof-of-work
nonce searched among the first `fuel`). Its correctness is proved (`lean_vm/LeanVM/Prover/`: the
prover's proof is, at every permutation, the proof of the honest strategy the completeness proof
follows, at the execution's own witness), so a reviewer need not read it line by line; a reviewer
checks only that it is a genuine prover: it computes its proof from the execution, its only search
is the nonce search bounded by `fuel`, and it branches on no permutation answer other than through
the challenges the verifier's program samples (`Run.lean` gives the script nothing else).

The knowledge extractor is not part of the statement: `Statement.Theorems` takes it as a
cost-machine program (`CostMachine.lean`). A program excludes classical choice; its size bound
(`14400`, the compiled `13813` and a slack of about one leanXMSS signature) leaves the compiled
extractor no room for a useful table of witnesses; and its budget is `2^95 + O(N·log³N)` for
`N` permutation queries (polylogarithmic per query: about `2^95` for `N ≤ 2^64`, `2^157.08` at
`N = 2^128`, where the soundness bound is already trivial). `lean_vm` names the program (`LeanVM.Cost.extractorProgram`, its extractor
`transcriptExtractor` compiled, `lean_vm/LeanVM/Protocol/Extraction/Extractor.lean`). The address
indices and the public boundary it shares with the honest prover (`powIndex`, `gLogIn`, `stepIn`,
`boundaryOK`) are in `Prover/Witness.lean`.

A few definitions carry small proofs, which are here because the definitions need them:
decidability instances, `Fin` index bounds (`by omega`, `by decide`), and termination arguments
(the cost machine's evaluator, `LeanDa.lean`'s tree levels).

Each project's root module (`lean_vm/LeanVM.lean`, `lean_xmss/XmssSecurity.lean`,
`lean_sphincs/Sphincs.lean`, `lean_da/LeanDa.lean`) is its end file: it checks that each end
theorem proves its statement and pins its axioms to Lean's three (`propext`, `Classical.choice`,
`Quot.sound`). The guest program's two soundness statements take the decoder as a parameter, a
cost-machine program within a size bound and a budget (`Theorems.lean`). Their content is in
that decoder: one that output another statement of the same digest would satisfy
`AggregateSound` (`Corollaries.lean`) through a Keccak-256 collision, which it would exhibit; what
excludes it is that none is known. The proofs' decoder, `LeanVM.Guest.End.decode`
([`EndFinal.lean`](../lean_vm/LeanVM/Guest/EndFinal.lean)), replays the run and reads the witness
off the frames it entered; the statement asks only that the program compute the witness from the
execution within its budget. The program is that decoder compiled (`LeanVM.Cost.decoderProgram`).

## The guest decoder's size bound and budget

**The size bound** (`guestDecoderSizeBound`, `786000`) limits any program; it is the compiled
decoder's size, `785533` (proved at most that and measured exactly), and a slack of `467`: room for
one leanXMSS signature's encoding (`309`) beside its code but not two, so no useful table of
witnesses (hard-coded signatures masking a bug of the guest program on finitely many statements)
fits there. That size counts the guest program's per-function tables, literals of the program, in
full. It does not bound what a program written differently could hold: the guest's frame-cell table
(`749835` of the size, cells below `2^20`) packs to about a third, three cells to a limb, freeing
room for about `1600` signatures' encodings.

**The budget** (`guestDecoderBudget`) is a short formula, `(steps + 1)·(2^21 + 2^13·lg) +
(70κ + 374)·2^κ + 2^37·(lg + 16)` with `lg = log₂(steps + 1)`, above the closed-form price
`lean_vm` proves of the compiled decoder (`lean_vm/LeanVM/Cost/Prices.lean`, within `0.16` bits of
it): a price per step of the run (the replay and the filing of the state in the table of function
entries, its share of one sort, `2^20.9` to `2^21.1`, growing as `log(steps)`); the sorted table of
the memory's addresses, at the execution's `κ`; and the witness read at the counts and the cursors
a valid execution bounds (about `2^41`: the guest's own asserts give at most `16` children, `65535`
claims of each scheme and `65535` coverage slots, `valid_counts`; each child's cursor, a prefix of
its proof's stream, is at most `8192` words, since the deferring verifier accepts that proof on a
valid execution (`valid_cursor`); the decoder clamps `da_verify`'s row count at `1024`), with about
`2^26.57` frame lookups. It is `2^42.74` at `κ = 20` and `2^20` steps, `2^45.32` at `κ = 24` and
`2^24`, `2^49.17` at `κ = 28` and `2^28`, `2^51.17` at `κ = 32` and `2^30`, `2^72.26` at `κ = 32`
and `2^51`, `2^81.30` at `κ = 32` and `2^60`. A valid execution of the guest program has fewer than
`2^51` steps: the memory is fixed and every instruction reads a cell at `fp·o` for an offset `o`
fixed by `pc`, so at each `pc` at most `2^κ` values of `fp` let a step succeed; the run is
determined by `(pc, fp)`, and a repeated state would loop without halting (`2^ℓ·2^κ` states,
`ℓ = 19`, `κ ≤ 32`; argued, not a Lean theorem; `lean_vm` proves fewer than `2^128`,
`steps_lt_replayFuel`).

## Building

`lake build` here builds the fifteen files, the data, `Fast/` and `Prover/`; the other projects
build it as a dependency. The data's modules are heavy: `python3 -I ../lake-build.py .` builds them
in one `lake build` that admits its modules by memory, as CI does.
