# Statement

What the repository proves, in the files a reviewer reads: definitions and end statements only,
one Lake package (`statement`) of seventeen files, `Statement/<Name>.lean` (Lean modules
`Statement.<Name>`), the leanISA guest program's generated bytecode in [`Data/`](Data/), and, for
speed only, proved `@[csimp]` lemmas in [`Fast/`](Fast/), which a reviewer need not read, and the
honest prover's definitions in [`Prover/`](Prover/), which a reviewer checks only for being a
genuine prover (below). It imports Mathlib, VCVio and CompPoly and nothing else (CI checks it);
every other project of the repository is proof and imports it.

**The model.** Every end statement with an adversary is in the random-permutation model: one
uniformly random permutation `π` of the 1600-bit Keccak state, which every party queries forward
(`π x`) and inverse (`π⁻¹ y`), Keccak-256 the sponge over `π` and Keccak-f[1600] `π` itself. That
the deployed Keccak-f[1600], a fixed permutation, behaves as a random one is the assumption,
even to programs that compute it: in the model the `KECCAK` instruction a proved program executes
computes the deployed Keccak-f[1600], independent of the transcript's `π`, which excludes by
construction programs that evaluate their own proof's Fiat-Shamir duplex (the setting of the known
attacks on Fiat-Shamir for GKR-based systems, Khovratovich, Rothblum and Soukhanov, 2025). The
adversary is classical, its code fixed before `π` is sampled; quantum adversaries are not covered.
`Statement/` contains no random oracle: the random-oracle model and the sponge's
indifferentiability, through which the proofs pass, are proof steps, and their definitions
(the two-oracle and duplex simulators, Wagner's reductions and his random-oracle terms) are in
`common` and `lean_da`. LeanDA's code binding is conditional on the argument's knowledge
soundness in the random-permutation model, the only term of its bound that depends on the
argument; an argument whose relation uses the deployed Keccak-f[1600] instead of `π` (leanVM
proving the guest program's DA check) cannot meet that hypothesis in this model.

**The sources.** The leanVM definitions are transcribed from leanVM at
`542b0353aa2ab87602f5a04455a985d099c9765c` (branch `main`, [`../leanvm-rev`](../leanvm-rev)): each
cites its source (`verifier.py:N`, `*.rs:N`, `doc/leanvm` sections). LeanDA's security framework is
Benedikt Wagner's ([b-wagn/fv-pq-da](https://github.com/b-wagn/fv-pq-da), Apache-2.0, see
[`../lean_da/NOTICE`](../lean_da/NOTICE)).

**What is trusted.** That the deployed Keccak-f[1600] behaves as a random permutation; that no one
finds a Keccak-256 collision (the guest program's statements); that the cost machine's prices are
honest (each step at least `1` and at least the words it writes; a carry-less product of one-limb
operands one instruction, x86-64's PCLMULQDQ or ARMv8's PMULL); that the leanVM definitions
transcribe their sources; that the Rust verifier's expansion of the pruned Merkle paths the Rust
prover ships into the full paths the statement's proofs carry is right (`LeanVm.lean`, not
formalized); and that `Data/`, the guest program's bytecode, is leanVM's (CI regenerates it,
`guest-export --check`, and ties the deployed seed to the Rust's, `node-vectors`). **The outer
verifier** (`EthereumProof::verify`, `aggregation.rs:631-650`) checks of a root more than any end
statement models: it recomputes the root's deferred claim (`AggregateSound` assumes that it holds,
`DeferredClaim.HoldsWithShape`), derives the DA digest's membership challenge from the root (no
end statement does: `MatrixWitness.Opens` accepts any challenge the digest binds, the zero one
included, whose dual codeword is all ones, so the DA part of a node's relation says nothing of the
blob without that derivation),
and checks that the signer lists are sorted, without repeats and shorter than `2^16` (not
modelled). Even with these, a claim held through a child carries knowledge only if the children's
fresh claims are true, which needs the batching sound inside the circuit at every level: no end
statement gives it (`LeanVmGuest.lean`). Accepted trade-offs: the statement does not name the extractor or decoder
program (only their sizes and budgets); the decoder computes the witness from the execution; the
extracted execution need not be the committed one; the decoder's budget has no cap on steps, grows
as `steps·log steps`, and its constant (`2^42.17` to `2^44.30` at the points below, the address
tables at the execution's `κ` included) prices the witness at the bounds a valid execution's asserts
and its children's accepted proofs give (`16` children, each with `65535` claims of each scheme and
a cursor of `8192` words, `65535` declared claims of each scheme, `1024` rows of the DA matrix), not
at the run's, so with fewer children and claims, at `κ ≤ 28` and well below `2^22` steps, the budget
is several times the compiled decoder's price (at `κ = 32` the address tables, priced at the run's
`κ`, are most of it); that a valid execution of the guest program has fewer than `2^51` steps is
argued in prose, not proved in Lean; literal tables count toward size; the size bounds limit
programs written like the compiled ones (a decoder written differently could pack the guest's tables
tighter, or derive them from its input, room for between about `1600` and `2400` signatures); the
extractor's budget is `2^81 + O(N·log³N)` for `N` queries, which passes `2^128` at about `N = 2^100`
while the soundness bound is still `2^-28` there, and whose constant `2^81` would pay for a search of
about `2^70` Keccak-f[1600] evaluations, so knowledge of a witness that such a search finds (of low
entropy) is not what the statement certifies.

## Reading order

The entry point is [`Theorems.lean`](Theorems.lean) (item 16): skim it first, for the list of end
theorems, then read the files in this order.

1. [`Keccak.lean`](Keccak.lean): Keccak-f[1600] as leanVM computes it (`keccakF1600`, on
   `KeccakState`, `25` lanes of `64` bits: the one state type of the statement) and Keccak-256
   over any permutation of the state in any monad (`KeccakHash.keccak256With`), the deployed one
   over Keccak-f[1600] (`keccak256`). No hash is an oracle or a parameter of its own: every party
   hashes with Keccak-256 over the permutation it is given (`keccak256With`), `π` in the model.
2. [`RandomPermutation.lean`](RandomPermutation.lean): the random-permutation model
   (`permutationOracleSpec`: `π` and `π⁻¹`; `lazyRandomPermutation`, the lazily sampled uniform
   permutation); a cost bounded on every path (`QueryCostAtMost`); the oracles of the model
   (`RandomPermutationOracles`: coins, `π` and `π⁻¹` on `KeccakState`, nothing else;
   `queryPermutation`, a call of `π`), a game's cost in calls of `π` and `π⁻¹`
   (`permutationCallCost`), its run against the lazy permutation (`runWithRandomPermutation`) and a
   computation's queries recorded (`loggedOracles`, its transcript `OracleQueryLog`).
3. [`SufCma.lean`](SufCma.lean): the SUF-CMA game of a hash-based signature scheme over `π`
   (`SufCma.forgeryGame`) and its security notion (`SufCma.StronglyUnforgeable`), for one key.
4. [`HashSignatures.lean`](HashSignatures.lean): what leanXMSS and leanSPHINCS share (namespace
   `HashSig`): the tweakable hash's types, bytes and address (`crates/signatures/src/tweak.rs`),
   the address with its hypertree layer and tree, zero for leanXMSS), the hash `H`, Keccak-256 over
   a permutation `π` (`OverPermutation.keccakHash π`), and a hash
   chain, a Merkle path's fold and a Merkle tree's node (`walkChain`, `foldPath`, `buildTree`),
   which every chain, fold and tree of both schemes instantiates.
5. [`LeanXmss.lean`](LeanXmss.lean): the leanXMSS scheme over a permutation `π`, on the shared
   part, and `LeanXmss.RandomPermutationModel.LeanXmssStronglyUnforgeable` (`StronglyUnforgeable` at
   127 bits, `Q` counting every permutation call of the game, key generation and the honest signer's
   worst-case grinding included: about `2^-62` at a full lifetime).
6. [`LeanSphincs.lean`](LeanSphincs.lean): the leanSPHINCS scheme (on the shared part; FORS of
   24 trees of height 10 under a hypertree of three WOTS+C layers of heights 8, 8, 10, its signature
   the randomizer, the FORS opening and each layer's counter, chain values and path) with the
   deterministic signer, and `Sphincs.LeanSphincsStronglyUnforgeable` (`StronglyUnforgeable` at 127
   bits up to `1,182,000,000` signatures, `Q` counting the honest signer's worst-case grinding too;
   a key used past its signature limit is not covered).
7. [`Field.lean`](Field.lean): `K = GF(2^64)` (CompPoly's `BF64`), `E = GF(2^192)`, the generator
   `g`; Keccak lanes, bytes and digests as field elements; the Fiat-Shamir duplex sponge over a
   permutation (`Sponge`, `absorb`, `squeeze`), which leanVM's verifier and LeanDA's membership
   challenge share.
8. [`LeanDa.lean`](LeanDa.lean): LeanDA (fields, Reed–Solomon rows, the Merkle commitment, a tree
   per row over its cells then one over the rows' roots, the membership test on one challenge), the parts of Benedikt Wagner's framework the end statements name (the
   consistency of opened cells), the relation of LeanDA's argument (the root's challenge drawn by
   the deployed duplex over a permutation), arguments that query the model's oracles, the commitment
   and the challenge over a permutation (the challenge by `Field.lean`'s sponge), LeanDA's games in the random-permutation model,
   where the adversary, the argument (`OracleArgument`, its extractor reading the adversary's
   transcript) and the verifier query the same oracles (`RandomPermutationOracles`, hashing with
   Keccak-256 over `π`, `keccakDigestBytes queryPermutation`; position binding, code binding, the
   argument's knowledge soundness `knowledgeSoundnessGame`, completeness for every permutation `π`),
   and `LeanDa.LeanDaPerfectlyComplete`, `LeanDa.LeanDaPositionBinding`, `LeanDa.LeanDaCodeBinding`.
   Code binding is conditional: if the argument is knowledge sound in the random-permutation model
   with error `(q + c) · 2^-k`, LeanDA is code binding with error
   `(Q + c + 2^22) · 2^-k + (Q + 2^22) · 2^-178 + (Q + 2^22)² · 2^-247` for every `Q` and `r ≤ 1024`
   rows (a straight-line proof on Wagner's framework); the last term passes 1 at about
   `Q = 2^123.5`. `LeanDaCodeBinding`'s docstring says which arguments satisfy the hypothesis.
9. [`LeanIsa.lean`](LeanIsa.lean): leanISA (memory, the six instructions, programs, one step, `run`,
   `ValidExecution`: a run from the initial state `(pcInitial, fpInitial)` that halts in the final
   state `(pcFinal, fpFinal)`, the instruction counts `Execution.opcodeCount`, the bytecode
   encoding, the bytecode table the verifier reads, `bytecodeWords`, and the public input's digest,
   `publicInputDigest`, with its inverse, `PublicInput.ofDigest`), and a program assembled from
   compiled functions (`CompiledFunction`, `assemble`).
10. [`LeanVmVerifier.lean`](LeanVmVerifier.lean): the verifier, `python-verifier/verifier.py`
    transcribed section by section, over any permutation in any monad, Keccak-256 the sponge over it
    (`verifyWith`), with the WHIR constants it reads, the Keccak GKR reduction, the Fiat-Shamir
    transcript (`Field.lean`'s duplex sponge, Merkle openings), and the deferring verifier an
    aggregate runs on its children. Where the transcription's form differs from the pin's without
    changing what it computes, its docstring gives the pin's lines and why the two agree.
11. [`LeanVm.lean`](LeanVm.lean): a statement (a program and a public input), the verifier on it
   over a permutation (`leanVmVerifier π`), the honest prover's type and **the honest prover**,
   `leanVmHonestProver`
   (`LeanVM.Prover.prove` of [`Prover/`](Prover/)), the committed size; the random-permutation
   experiment (`knowledgeSoundnessExperiment`, one game: an adversary with private coins,
   `RandomPermutationAdversary`, its queries to `π` and `π⁻¹` counted by `permutationCallCost` and
   recorded, then the verifier over `π`), a straight-line extractor's type (`StraightLineExtractor`:
   from the statement, the transcript and the proof, an execution or none), the transcript's
   permutation queries (`permutationQueries`), **`LeanVM.KnowledgeSoundness extractor`** (for a
   given extractor; it asks for some valid execution, not necessarily the one the proof commits to),
   **`LeanVM.Completeness`**.
12. [`CostMachine.lean`](CostMachine.lean): the cost machine (`MachineValue`, `PrimitiveOperation`,
    `Term`, `MachineProgram`, `evaluateTerm`, `run`, a program's `size`, and `outputWithin`, a
    program's output within a size bound and a budget): a first-order functional language on natural
    numbers and arrays, each step priced by its algorithm, at least `1` and at least the words it
    writes; the encoding of the extractor's input and output (`encodeLeanVmKnowledgeExtractorInput`,
    `decodeExecution`, a linear parse); the extractor's size bound
    (`leanVmKnowledgeExtractorSizeBound`) and budget (`leanVmKnowledgeExtractorBudget N`, for `N`
    permutation queries in the transcript: the most its run may cost, so also the most steps it may
    execute); and **`LeanVM.extractorComputedBy P`**: the extractor a program `P` computes, its
    output parsed, if `P`'s size and its run's price are within them. `Statement.Theorems` takes `P`
    as a parameter and does not name it (`lean_vm` names its knowledge extractor, compiled). That
    the machine's prices are honest is trusted. The size bound is `14500`: the compiled extractor's
    `14109` and a slack of about one leanXMSS signature's encoding, so the compiled extractor could
    carry no useful table of witnesses beside its code. The budget is a short formula,
    `2^81 + (N + 2^30)·(D + 2)²·(2^15 + 2D)` with `D = log₂(N + 2) + 1`: about `2^81` for `N ≤ 2^50`
    (the list decoder, about `2^80.49` at the worst shape) and `2^157.08` at `N = 2^128` (the
    transcript's index); `lean_vm` proves the compiled extractor's price below it at every shape the
    replay admits. **What is trusted** says what the budget's regime leaves out.
13. [`LeanVmGuest.lean`](LeanVmGuest.lean): what a node of the aggregation proves: its statement
    (`NodeStatement`: leanXMSS and leanSPHINCS claims, a DA digest, a claim it defers on the guest
    program's bytecode) and the statement's digest under a seed (`statementDigest`, Keccak-256 of
    `statementPreimage`, the node's public input); its witnesses (`NodeWitness`: children carrying
    proofs, raw signatures with their claims, the blob matrix it checks, the batching's hints),
    **`LeanVM.Node.NodeRelation`**, the witnesses holding the claims, as four conditions, each
    defined right after it (`SignatureClaimsHeld`: every raw signature verifies, and every leanXMSS
    and leanSPHINCS claim is a raw signature's or in a child's list; `DaDigestHeld`: the DA digest that of the blob matrix the node checks,
    zeros or a child's; `ChildrenProven`: every child's proof accepted by the deferring verifier;
    `DeferredClaimBatched`: the deferred claim the batching of the children's, and so of the
    bytecode's shape); then a decoding (`SeedStatementAndWitness`: a seed, a statement, a witness) and
    what it explains of a public input (`ExplainsPublicInput`), the candidate collisions of two
    statements' digests and **`LeanVM.Node.StatementDigestBinds`** (a digest binds the seed and a
    statement of a given shape, up to a Keccak-256 collision), the deployed leanISA guest
    program (`LeanVM.Guest.guestProgram`, from [`Data/`](Data/)), and the guest decoder as a
    cost-machine program `D`: the encodings of a decoding (`encodeValue`), `D`'s input
    (`encodeGuestWitnessDecoderInput`: the guest program and the execution), its size bound
    (`guestWitnessDecoderSizeBound`) and budget (`guestWitnessDecoderBudget`), and
    **`LeanVM.Node.GuestKnowledgeSoundness D`** (on every valid execution, `D`, run within its
    budget, outputs the encoding of a decoding that explains the public input; no parse). `D` may
    compute the witness by any algorithm (the proofs' decoder reads it off the memory), as the
    extractor program may compute an execution by any algorithm. Two caveats, in the module's
    docstring and `MatrixWitness.Opens`': a claim held through a child carries no knowledge by
    itself (the child's fresh claim, its proof's claim on the bytecode, comes from the witness), and
    the blob matrix opens a membership challenge of the witness's choosing (only the outer verifier's
    derivation of the root's challenge, not modelled, makes its rows codewords).
14. [`LeanVmGuestBatching.lean`](LeanVmGuestBatching.lean): the reference material
    `LeanVmGuest.lean` imports, transcribed with its citations: the deferred claim
    (`DeferredClaim`), and the batching of the children's deferred claims as a verifier program
    (`Batch.nodeBatchingProgram`, run from the batching's seed by `runBatching`).
15. [`LeanVmGuestCompleteness.lean`](LeanVmGuestCompleteness.lean): the guest program's deployed
    limits (`DeployedLimits`: the declared claims a sub-multiset of those made, at most `16`
    children, the coverage table's slots, `coverageSlots`, below `2^16`), the nodes the deployed
    budget covers (`CoveredNode`), the leanVM verifier refusing a zero challenge
    (`verifierRefusingZero`, `VerifierProgram.refusingZeroChallenge`),
    **`LeanVM.Guest.Completeness.HonestProofRarelyRefused`** (in the random-permutation model), a
    child carrying an honest proof (`HonestChild`) and
    **`LeanVM.Guest.Completeness.GuestCompleteOnHonestChildren`** (every covered node under the
    deployed seed whose children carry honest proofs has a valid execution of the guest program);
    and the recursion step: the batching of the children's claims as a verifier program
    (`boundedBatchingProgram`), an adversary's proof run through it over `π` in the
    random-permutation model (one game), the event that it accepts children with a false claim
    (`SomeChildClaimFalse`), the program's query bound (`batchingQueryBound`), and
    **`LeanVM.Guest.Batch.BatchingSound`**: the batching run outside the circuit with Keccak-f[1600]
    = `π`, which does not compose with the guest program's in-circuit statements (their `KECCAK` is
    the deployed Keccak-f[1600]), so soundness across recursion levels does not follow from it.
16. [`Theorems.lean`](Theorems.lean): every end theorem, as the fields of `Statement.Theorems`,
    for two cost-machine programs of the same type, so every use of `Statement.Theorems` names them
    (`(leanVmKnowledgeExtractor := P) (guestWitnessDecoder := D)`). The decoder's size bound,
    budget and their limits: see [The guest decoder's size bound and
    budget](#the-guest-decoders-size-bound-and-budget) and **What is trusted**.
17. [`Corollaries.lean`](Corollaries.lean), optional for review: what an accepted proof of the
    leanISA guest program establishes, `KnowledgeSoundness`, `GuestKnowledgeSoundness` and
    `StatementDigestBinds` composed (`LeanVM.Node.AggregateSound`, with `ExplainsStatement`), for a
    root statement whose deferred claim holds, as the outer verifier checks; `lean_vm` proves
    `Corollaries` from `Theorems` at the same two programs.

[`Data/`](Data/) is the guest program's compiled bytecode, generated from leanVM's sources by
`lean_vm/scripts/guest-export` (do not edit; `guest-export --check` fails when it is stale, as CI
runs it). It needs no review beyond that check.

[`Fast/`](Fast/) makes the compiled definitions fast and changes nothing they mean: `@[csimp]`
lemmas, each proved, that compile `GF(2^64)` products on machine words (`FieldWords`), the
degree-three extension's product unrolled (`CubicExt`), Keccak-f[1600] with its lanes written out
and Keccak-256 over any permutation with its blocks read from arrays (`Keccak`), and the signature
schemes' verifiers with a constant-depth lookup (`Schemes`). A `@[csimp]` lemma acts where a definition is compiled, so they are here,
imported by `Field.lean`, `HashSignatures.lean`, `LeanDa.lean` and `LeanVmGuest.lean`, for the
statement's own compiled definitions (the verifier, the prover, the signature schemes, the guest
program's specification, LeanDA) and every module after them. They need no review: a `@[csimp]`
lemma is an equation the kernel checks.

[`Prover/`](Prover/) is leanVM's honest prover as an executable program,
`LeanVM.Prover.prove F prog input t ρ fuel` (`Prove.lean`), over a permutation `F` in any monad,
Keccak-256 the sponge over `F`: over the model's `π` it is the prover `LeanVM.leanVmHonestProver`
that `Completeness`, `HonestProofRarelyRefused` and `HonestChild` name, and over Keccak-f[1600] in
`Id` it is `lake exe leanvm-prove`, whose proofs the Rust and Lean verifiers accept
(`lean_vm/README.md`). It is definitions only: the witness from the trace (`Witness.lean`,
`Columns.lean`: one run of the program; no proof, `none`, unless the trace is a valid execution
within `2^28` committed cells), a script of the prover's moves (`Script.lean`) for each phase of the
verifier (`Ntt.lean`, `Bus.lean`, `Gkr.lean`, `Table.lean`, `Prove.lean`'s public input,
`Keccak*.lean`, `Whir.lean`'s ring switching and opening; `Mle.lean`, `Code.lean` their helpers),
and its run against the verifier's own program (`Run.lean`: the challenges squeezed from the
sponge, each Merkle tree built once, the proof-of-work nonce searched among the first `fuel`). Its correctness is proved
(`lean_vm/LeanVM/Prover/`: the prover's proof is, at every permutation, the proof of the honest
strategy the completeness proof follows, at the execution's own witness), so a reviewer need not
read it line by line; a reviewer checks only that it is a genuine prover: it computes its proof from
the execution, its only search is the nonce search bounded by `fuel`, and it branches on no
permutation answer other than through the challenges the verifier's program samples (`Run.lean`
gives the script nothing else).

The knowledge extractor is not part of the statement: `Statement.Theorems` takes it as a
cost-machine program (`CostMachine.lean`). A program excludes classical choice; its size bound
(`14500`, the compiled `14109` and a slack of about one leanXMSS signature) leaves the compiled
extractor no room for a useful table of witnesses; and its budget is `2^81 + O(N·log³N)` for `N`
permutation queries (polylogarithmic per query: about `2^81` for `N ≤ 2^50`, `2^157.08` at
`N = 2^128`; **What is trusted** says what its regime leaves out). `lean_vm` names the program
(`LeanVM.Cost.compiledKnowledgeExtractor`, its extractor `transcriptExtractor` compiled,
`lean_vm/LeanVM/Protocol/Extraction/Extractor.lean`). The address indices and the public boundary it
shares with the honest prover (`powIndex`, `gLogIn`, `stepIn`, `boundaryOK`) are in
`Prover/Witness.lean`.

A few definitions carry small proofs, which are here because the definitions need them:
decidability instances, `Fin` index bounds (`by omega`, `by decide`), and termination arguments
(the cost machine's evaluator).

Each project's root module (`lean_vm/LeanVM.lean`, `lean_xmss/XmssSecurity.lean`,
`lean_sphincs/Sphincs.lean`, `lean_da/LeanDa.lean`) is its end file: it checks that each end
theorem proves its statement and pins its axioms to Lean's three (`propext`, `Classical.choice`,
`Quot.sound`). The guest program's two soundness statements take the decoder as a parameter, a
cost-machine program within a size bound and a budget (`LeanVmGuest.lean`). Their content is in
that decoder: one that output another statement of the same digest would satisfy
`AggregateSound` (`Corollaries.lean`) through a Keccak-256 collision, which it would exhibit; what
excludes it is that none is known. The proofs' decoder, `LeanVM.Guest.End.decode`
([`EndFinal.lean`](../lean_vm/LeanVM/Guest/EndFinal.lean)), replays the run and reads the witness
off the frames it entered; the statement asks only that the program compute the witness from the
execution within its budget. The program is that decoder compiled
(`LeanVM.Cost.compiledGuestWitnessDecoder`).

## The guest decoder's size bound and budget

**The size bound** (`guestWitnessDecoderSizeBound`, `786000`) limits any program; it is the compiled
decoder's size, `783705` (proved at most that and measured exactly), and a slack of `2295`: room for
seven leanXMSS signatures' encodings (`309` each) beside its code but not eight (the bound was set
at leanVM `c445e3d5`, where the slack was `439`, room for one), so no useful table of witnesses
(hard-coded signatures masking a bug of the guest program on finitely many statements) fits there. That size counts the guest program's per-function tables, literals of the program, in
full. It does not bound what a program written differently could hold: the guest's frame-cell table
(`748108` of the size, cells below `2^20`) packs to about a third, three cells to a limb, freeing
room for about `1600` signatures' encodings. Nor is the compiled program compact: its size also
counts, a literal `0` each, the indices without a body left by the numbering it shares with the
extractor's compiled functions; renumbered compactly, the same program would leave room for a few
signatures' encodings ([`lean_vm/README.md`](../lean_vm/README.md#the-guests-proof) has the
measurements). And a program may compute its tables from its input, which holds the guest program:
one that derived the frame-cell table at run time instead of carrying it would free most of those
`748108`, room for up to about `2400` signatures' encodings (an estimate, not measured).

**The budget** (`guestWitnessDecoderBudget`) is a short formula,
`(steps + 1)·(2^21 + 2^13·lg) + (70κ + 374)·2^κ + 2^37·(lg + 16)` with `lg = log₂(steps + 1)`, above
the closed-form price `lean_vm` proves of the compiled decoder (`lean_vm/LeanVM/Cost/Prices.lean`,
within `0.22` to `1.25` bits of it, about `0.55` to `0.65` at the six points below): a price per
step of the run (the replay and the filing of the state in the table of function entries, its share
of one sort, `2^20.41` to `2^20.75` against the budget's `2^21` to `2^21.3`, growing as
`log(steps)`); the sorted table of the memory's addresses, at the execution's `κ`; and the witness
read at the counts and the cursors a valid execution bounds (`2^37·(lg + 16)`, about `2^42` to
`2^43`: the guest's own asserts give at most `16` children, `65535` claims of each scheme and
`65535` coverage slots, `valid_counts`; each child's cursor, a prefix of its proof's stream, is at
most `8192` words, since the deferring verifier accepts that proof on a valid execution
(`valid_cursor`); the decoder clamps `da_verify`'s row count at `1024`), with about `2^26.57` frame
lookups. It is `2^42.74` at `κ = 20` and `2^20` steps, `2^45.32` at `κ = 24` and `2^24`, `2^49.17`
at `κ = 28` and `2^28`, `2^51.17` at `κ = 32` and `2^30`, `2^72.26` at `κ = 32` and `2^51`,
`2^81.30` at `κ = 32` and `2^60` (the price there: `2^42.09`, `2^44.74`, `2^48.60`, `2^50.61`,
`2^71.71`, `2^80.75`). A valid execution of the guest program has fewer than `2^51` steps: the
memory is fixed and every instruction reads a cell at `fp·o` for an offset `o` fixed by `pc`, so at
each `pc` at most `2^κ` values of `fp` let a step succeed; the run is determined by `(pc, fp)`, and
a repeated state would loop without halting (`2^ℓ·2^κ` states, `ℓ = 19`, `κ ≤ 32`; argued, not a
Lean theorem; `lean_vm` proves fewer than `2^128`, `steps_lt_replayFuel`).

## Building

`lake build` here builds the seventeen files, the data, `Fast/` and `Prover/`; the other projects
build it as a dependency. The data's modules are heavy: `python3 -I ../lake-build.py .` builds them
in one `lake build` that admits its modules by memory, as CI does.
