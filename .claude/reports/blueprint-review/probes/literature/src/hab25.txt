      A note on mutual correlated agreement for
                Reed–Solomon codes
                                  Ulrich Haböck
                              ulrich@starkware.co,
                                 November 17, 2025


                                          Abstract
          We outline how to generalize the Guruswami-Sudan list decoder anal-
      ysis from Ben–Sasson, Carmon, Ishai, Kopparty and Saraf [BCI+ 20] in
      order to obtain a “global” proximity gap, called mutual correlated agree-
      ment in [ACFY24], or strong correlated agreement in [Zei24].



1     Introduction

Let C = RS[Fq , D, k] be a Reed–Solomon code over a finite field Fq , with domain
of definition D ⊆ Fq , and dimension k + 1. Let C 2 be the interleaved code of C,
consisting of all words [p0 , p1 ] with p0 , p1 ∈ C. We write ∆(f, g) for the relative
Hamming distance between functions f, g : D → Fq , and we use the same
notation for interleaved words. If two interleaved words [f0 , f1 ], [g0 , g1 ] agree
on a set A of density α ∈ (0, 1), we say that they have correlated α-agreement.
In terms of the relative distance, ∆([f0 , f1 ], [g0 , g1 ]) ≤ 1 − α.

    For any f0 , f1 : D → Fq and distance γ ∈ (0, 1), we consider

             L([f0 , f1 ], γ) = {[p0 , p1 ] ∈ C 2 : ∆([p0 , p1 ], [f0 , f1 ]) ≤ γ},

be the set of all γ-proximate codewords of [f0 , f1 ]. This set is empty whenever
∆([f0 , f1 ], C 2 ) > γ, and non-emptiness implies that ∆([f0 , f1 ], C 2 ) ≤ γ, in other
words: the existence of some [p0 , p1 ] ∈ C 2 , which has correlated (1−γ)-agreement
with [f0 , f1 ].


Mutual correlated agreement. A regular proximity gap states that, if for
sufficiently many z ∈ Fq , the linear combination fz = f0 + zf1 is γ-close to C,
then ∆([f0 , f1 ], C 2 ) ≤ γ (and consequently ∆(fz , C) ≤ γ for every z). A global
proximity gap instead aspires the following rough statement: For all, but a few

    Mutual correlated agreement is a recent notion which is often used to sim-
plify the security analysis of interactive oracle proofs (or, proofs of proximities),
in particular those in which the proximate codewords are subject to certain
constraints. The notion was first considered useful in the soundness analysis of
Basefold [ZCF23], for which Zeilberger, Chen and Fish observed that it holds
for general linear codes up to θ = δ/3. An  √ early version of [Zei24] proves mu-
tual correlated agreement up to θ = 1 − 4 1 − δ (the “double” Johnson radius),
while concurrently
          √          Gao, Kan, and Li [GKL24] showed its validity even up to
θ = 1 − 3 1 − δ (the “one-and-a-half” Johnson bound).1 In the special case
of Reed-Solomon codes, Anon, Chiesa, Fenzi and Yogev [ACFY24] proved mu-
tual correlated agreement√ up to δ/2, and they conjectured it to hold up to the
Johnson radius θ = 1 − 1 − δ.

a multilinear manner, or using powers of z, can be proven similarly, without
additional technical obstacles.

2     From collinearity to correlated agreement

The following lemma, which goes back to Ames, Hazay, Ishai, Venkitasubrama-
niam [AHIV17] is used as the final step in many correlated agreement theorems,
for example [BKS18, BCI+ 20, Hab24, GKL24, Zei24, BCH+ 25]. It states that,
given a family of proximate polynomials which lie on a line, then we can in-
fer correlated agreement whenever the family is large enough. We present the
lemma in the lossless form, and for general linear codes.
Lemma 1 ([AHIV17, BKS18]). Given an arbitrary linear code C represented
by words over the domain D, and functions f0 , f1 : D → Fq . Fix γ ∈ (0, 1), and
assume two codewords p0 , p1 ∈ C, such that ∆(p0 + zp1 , f0 + zf1 ) ≤ γ for all z
contained in a set S ⊆ Fq . Then, if

                                    |S| > ⌈γ · n⌉ + 1,

it holds that ∆([a, b], [f0 , f1 ]) ≤ γ. In other words, [f0 , f1 ] agrees with [a, b] ∈ C 2
over a set of density 1 − γ.

Proof. Without loss of generality, we assume that γ = e/n for some integer e ∈
[0, n], where n is the size of the domain D. We show that ∆([a, b], [f1 , f2 ]) > γ
leads to a contradiction, proving the claim of the lemma.

    Assume e < n · ∆([a, b], [f1 , f2 ]). Then the disagreement set

                   E = {x ∈ D : (p0 (x), p1 (x)) ̸= (f0 (x), f1 (x))}

contains a subset E ′ of size e + 1. Denote fz = f0 + zf1 and cz = p0 + zp1 .
For any scalar z ∈ S we have ∆(fz , cz ) ≤ e/n, hence there must be at least
one x ∈ E ′ for which cz (x) = fz (x). On the other hand, each point x from the
disagreement set E has at most one z ∈ S for which cz (x) = fz (x). In fact, by
linearity of the selection,

                cz (x) − fz (x) = p0 (x) − f0 (x) + z · (p1 (x) − f1 (x)),

is a non-trivial linear functional, since (p0 (x), p1 (x)) ̸= (f0 (x), f1 (x)). Therefore,

                                   |S| ≤ |E ′ | = e + 1,

in contradiction to the size assumption on S.

K = Fq (Z) the field of rational functions over Fq , and its analysis requires
certain familiarity with algebraic function fields.
