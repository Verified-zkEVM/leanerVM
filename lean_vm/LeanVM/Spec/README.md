# The specification

What the project proves, in the files a reviewer reads. They import nothing else from the project (CI checks it); the rest is proof. The sources are leanVM's at `3375f7a137c50b803f3416f6466b5d4e0719708a` (branch `keccak-gkr`).

Read in this order:

1. [`Field.lean`](Field.lean): the fields `K = GF(2^64)` and `E = GF(2^192)`, and the generator `g`.
2. [`Keccak.lean`](Keccak.lean): the permutation Keccak-f[1600] (`keccakF`), Keccak-256 of bytes (Ethereum's padding, `keccak256`), a digest as four 64-bit words, the byte encodings the verifier hashes and a digest as two field elements.
3. [`Isa.lean`](Isa.lean): leanISA (programs, memory, one step, valid executions, the instructions of each opcode an execution executes, among them `KECCAK`, Keccak-f on cells) and the encoding of an instruction.
4. [`Verifier.lean`](Verifier.lean): the verifier, transcribed from leanVM's Python verifier, with the WHIR constants it reads, the Keccak GKR reduction, and the Fiat-Shamir transcript it runs on (a duplex sponge over Keccak-f[1600]); and the deferring verifier an aggregate runs on its children, which takes the value of the fixed polynomial, the bytecode, from a claim instead of computing it. The prover announces each table's row count, the table's height being the least power of two holding them; the bus discounts the rows past the count (padding rows) through a public term (`paddingWeight`, `Table.padding`).
5. [`Extractor.lean`](Extractor.lean): the knowledge extractor, straight-line: from the proof and the oracle's answers to the adversary's queries (not the verifier's), the announced sizes and the stack's root (read off the proof as the verifier's first two steps read them), the word the root commits to (the Merkle tree inverted through the adversary's answers), the candidates (the stacks whose Reed–Solomon encoding agrees with the word on at least level 0's Johnson threshold of columns), and the halting run of a candidate's memory image that is a valid execution, if there is one.
6. [`Statement.lean`](Statement.lean): the random-oracle experiment, with two oracles, Keccak-256 on byte strings and Keccak-f[1600] on states (`verifier H F`), recording the oracle's answers when the adversary returns, which the extractor reads; and the two statements, `KnowledgeSoundness` (for the extractor of `Extractor.lean`) and `Completeness`.
7. [`Rpm.lean`](Rpm.lean): the same two statements in the random-permutation model, on `lean_rpm`'s lazily sampled permutation `π` of the Keccak state (not a `module`: it imports `lean_rpm`): Keccak-256 the sponge over `π`, Keccak-f[1600] `π`, the adversary querying `π` and `π⁻¹` with its transcript recorded; `KnowledgeSoundnessRpm` for the extractor of `Extractor.lean` on the Keccak-256 evaluations of the transcript (`transcriptHashes`, `lean_rpm`'s `pathsOf` on the states' bytes), and `CompletenessRpm`.

The aggregation guest (`crates/rec_aggregation`): what a proof of it would establish is stated below, but at this pin it is not proved (the BLAKE2s guest's proofs, `LeanVM/Guest/`, were not carried over; see the project README):

8. The two signature schemes, leanXMSS and leanSPHINCS, imported from the projects that prove their security, here with Keccak-256 as their hash: [`lean_xmss/LeanXmss/Scheme.lean`](../../../lean_xmss/LeanXmss/Scheme.lean) and [`lean_sphincs/Sphincs/Scheme.lean`](../../../lean_sphincs/Sphincs/Scheme.lean).
9. [`Da.lean`](Da.lean): the claim on a DA root, in terms of LeanDA as [`lean_da/LeanDa/Scheme.lean`](../../../lean_da/LeanDa/Scheme.lean) defines it (the deployed commitment, the membership vector and its two-level digest), on this project's Keccak-256 and, for the challenges' sponge, Keccak-f[1600].
10. [`Node.lean`](Node.lean): a node's statement and its Keccak-256 digest, the deferred claim on the bytecode (a point and a value), the batching of the children's claims (one sumcheck over the duplex sponge), `NodeRel` and `NodeSpec` (what a node establishes, with a witness and with some witness) and the outer verifier.
11. [`Guest.lean`](Guest.lean): a decoder (a seed, a statement and a witness read off a memory), and what a proof of the guest establishes with it: `AggregateSound` (what the outer verifier's acceptance of a statement establishes, the headline), `GuestKnowledge` (every valid execution decodes to a statement of its input's digest and a witness holding it); the Keccak-256 collision candidates of two statements' digests.

The same statements with the hash, the permutation and the `KECCAK` relation as parameters, each recovering the deployed one (bridge lemmas `…_deployed`, `…_id`), for proofs about the guest:

12. [`IsaWith.lean`](IsaWith.lean): leanISA with the relation `KECCAK` checks a parameter (`KeccakSem`): the deployed one (`keccakDeployed`, Keccak-f[1600] on the cells) and the reduction-blind one (`keccakFree`, canonical cells).
13. [`NodeWith.lean`](NodeWith.lean) and [`DaWith.lean`](DaWith.lean): the node's digests, signature verification, batching (from a sponge state) and DA claim with the hash and the permutation in any monad, and `NodeRelWith`, `Witness.OpensWith` over an answered (`Option`-valued) hash and permutation.

[`Fast/`](Fast/) needs no review: it holds `@[csimp]` theorems, each proved, that make the compiled field arithmetic, Keccak-f[1600] and Keccak-256, LeanDA's membership vector and the signature schemes' verifiers fast. They change no definition.

The proofs are `LeanVM.knowledgeSoundness_holds` in [`LeanVM/Protocol/LeanVMSound.lean`](../Protocol/LeanVMSound.lean) and `LeanVM.completeness_holds` in [`LeanVM/Protocol/LeanVM.lean`](../Protocol/LeanVM.lean), and `LeanVM.knowledgeSoundnessRpm_holds` and `LeanVM.completenessRpm_holds` in [`LeanVM/Protocol/LeanVMRpm.lean`](../Protocol/LeanVMRpm.lean).
