# lean_vm

A Lean 4 proof that leanVM's verifier is knowledge sound and complete in the random-permutation model, for the verifier, the instruction set and the arithmetization of the leanVM commit in [`../leanvm-rev`](../leanvm-rev): Keccak-256 and Keccak-f[1600] are both built from one uniformly random permutation `π` of the Keccak state, which the adversary queries forward and inverse.

## What is proved

The statements are in [`Statement/`](../Statement/README.md), the repository's statement package, which imports only Mathlib, VCVio and CompPoly (CI checks it): everything in this project is proof. Its files for leanVM, in reading order: [`Field.lean`](../Statement/Field.lean) (the fields), [`LeanIsa.lean`](../Statement/LeanIsa.lean) (leanISA: memory, the six instructions, programs, one step, `ValidExecution`, a run from the initial state `(pcInitial, fpInitial) = (1, 1)` that halts in the final state `(pcFinal, fpFinal) = (g^(N_prog - 1), 1)`, `Trace.count`), [`LeanVmVerifier.lean`](../Statement/LeanVmVerifier.lean) (the verifier, `python-verifier/verifier.py` transcribed section by section, for any hash function and permutation), [`Extractor.lean`](../Statement/Extractor.lean) (the knowledge extractor), [`LeanVm.lean`](../Statement/LeanVm.lean) (a statement and the verifier on it, the errors, the random-permutation model and **the two statements, `KnowledgeSoundness` and `Completeness`**, below) and [`LeanVmGuest.lean`](../Statement/LeanVmGuest.lean) (the aggregation guest: the DA claim, a node's relation `NodeRel`, `GuestKnowledge`, `AggregateSound`, the deployed guest and its completeness on the covered nodes), after [`RandomPermutation.lean`](../Statement/RandomPermutation.lean) (Keccak and the model). The `@[csimp]` theorems that make the compiled field arithmetic, Keccak and the schemes' verifiers fast are in [`Statement/Fast/`](../Statement/Fast/), where the statement's own compiled definitions see them; [`LeanVM/Facts/`](LeanVM/Facts/) holds the lemmas about the statement's definitions that the statement itself does not need (`…_deployed`, `…_id`), and the definitions no end statement reaches, moved out of the statement with their names: the `…With` variants (any hash and permutation, any `KECCAK` semantics), the deployed `Verifier.verify` and `seed`, a node's `work` and a child's `childShape`.

The proofs are `LeanVM.knowledgeSoundness_holds` ([`LeanVM/Protocol/LeanVMRpm.lean`](LeanVM/Protocol/LeanVMRpm.lean)) and `LeanVM.completeness_holds` ([`LeanVM/Prover/End.lean`](LeanVM/Prover/End.lean)).

**The model.** One permutation `π` of the 1600-bit Keccak state, uniformly random (`Common.lazyPerm` of [`common`](../common), lazily sampled), queried forward (`π x`) and inverse (`π⁻¹ y`). Keccak-256 (Merkle hashes, the IV, the bytecode and statement digests) is the sponge over `π` (`keccak256With π`: `keccak256` with `π` for Keccak-f[1600]), and the Fiat-Shamir duplex sponge's permutation (the seed `π(iv ‖ public input ‖ 0)`, then one permutation per challenge and per full rate of absorbed scalars) is `π` itself (`rpmImpl`). The experiment (`rpmExperiment`) runs the adversary (`RpmAdversary`), which flips private coins, queries `π` and `π⁻¹` and outputs a statement (a program and a public input) and a proof, recording its queries to `π` and `π⁻¹` and their answers (`Transcript`), not its coins; then the verifier on its output, against the same permutation. Inside the executed program, the `KECCAK` instruction's Keccak-f[1600] is the deployed permutation, proved (it is the instruction's semantics, not a call to `π`). That the deployed Keccak-f[1600], a fixed permutation, behaves as a random one is the model's assumption.

**Knowledge soundness** (`KnowledgeSoundness`) is stated for one extractor, `extractorRpm`, which is straight-line: from the proof and the adversary's transcript alone (not the verifier's queries, not `π`'s other values; no rewinding) it reads the Keccak-256 evaluations the transcript contains (`hashesOf (transcriptPaths T)`: the byte strings whose whole padded sponge path the transcript holds as a chain of forward queries from the zero state, as `common`'s indifferentiability simulator books them, `Common.pathsOf`), and runs `LeanVM.Extraction.extract` ([`Statement/Extractor.lean`](../Statement/Extractor.lean)) on them: it reads the announced sizes and the stack's Merkle root off the proof as the verifier's first two steps do, reads the committed word off the evaluations by inverting the Merkle tree, and returns the halting run of the memory image of a stack in the word's Johnson list (its Reed–Solomon encoding agreeing with the word on at least level 0's Johnson threshold of columns) that is a valid execution, if one is. The extractor is deterministic and computable: it lists the stacks of the Johnson list (at most `2^18`, `LeanVM.candidates_ncard_le`) with CompPoly's Guruswami–Sudan list decoder over `GF(2^64)` on each lane and pruning across the lanes, and runs each memory image for at most as many steps as the announced tables have rows; that it finds a valid execution whenever the proof's own extractor does is `extractor_of_extracted` ([`LeanVM/Protocol/Extraction/`](LeanVM/Protocol/Extraction/)). Its running time is polynomial in the announced committed size `2^μ` and the number of evaluations (not in the proof's size), read off its loops rather than proved, and far too large at deployed sizes except on a word without errors. The statement puts no condition on the program: the machine halts in the final state `(g^(N_prog - 1), 1)`, the last slot in `main`'s frame, both registers (leanVM `990b9352`, `execute.rs:551-562`, specification §2), and the last slot reached in another frame executes like any other, so the walk the state bus balances, cut at its first visit of the final state, is a run (`exists_run_of_balanced`).

An adversary of at most `Q ≤ 2^128` queries to `π` and `π⁻¹` (its coins not counted, `permQueries`) breaks knowledge soundness with probability at most `(Q + 2^14) · 2^-128 + 5 (Q + 2^14)² · 2^-256` (`LeanVM.knowledgeSoundness_holds`, the end statement `KnowledgeSoundness`, from `LeanVM.Rpm.soundnessRpm_le_128`); for `Q ≤ 2^120` that is at most `(Q + 2^14) · 2^-127` (`LeanVM.Rpm.soundnessRpm_le_bits`). The proof's exact error is `soundnessError Q + (Q + 12006 · 2^32)^2 / 2^508`:

* `soundnessError Q = (Q + 12006)(2^-128 + 5(Q + 12006)/2^256) + 12006/2^256` (`12006` bounds the verifier's hash and permutation calls, proved over every admissible shape and not tight: it charges every WHIR opening at the largest commitment height; the `5` counts the digests and sponge capacities a fresh answer may collide with or predict; the last term pays for a fresh Keccak-256 answer to one of the verifier's calls equal to a Merkle root the adversary announced without computing it, which the extractor, reading only the adversary's evaluations, would miss);
* `(Q + 12006 · 2^32)^2 / 2^508` (`rpmError`) for the permutation calls of the whole experiment, the adversary's `Q` and at most `verifierPermCalls = 12006 · 2^32` of the verifier (each call at most `⌊(2^39 + 9) / 136⌋ + 1 ≤ 2^32` blocks: the longest input is the IV's preimage, the 9-byte `TAG` and the bytecode, `2^39 + 9` bytes at `2^32` instructions; a Merkle leaf has at most `192` words).

For `Q ≤ 2^120`, `5(Q + 12006) ≤ 2^128` and the second term is at most `2^242 / 2^508`, so the error is at most `(Q + 2^14) · 2^-127`; it is at most `2^-63` for `Q ≤ 2^64` (`LeanVM.Rpm.soundnessRpm_le`).

**Completeness** (`Completeness`) covers every valid execution within the verifier's size cap: its committed size (`Trace.committedSize`, the cells the prover commits to, set by the memory, the bytecode and how many instructions of each opcode it executes, the `KECCAK` table at least `2^9` rows and `q_keccak` `2^6` cells per row) at most `2^28`. The honest prover, `LeanVM.prover` (the executable `LeanVM.Prover.prove` of [`Statement/Prover/`](../Statement/Prover/) with the oracle calls; [the executable prover](#the-executable-prover)), at an inverse rate `2^ρ` with `1 ≤ ρ ≤ 4` and trying at most `fuel ≤ 2^64` nonces per proof of work, has its proof rejected with probability at most `completenessError fuel + ((12008 + 48017 · (2^27 + 2·fuel + 2)) · 2^32)^2 / 2^508` (`LeanVM.Rpm.completenessRpm`), where `completenessError fuel = Q · (1 - 2^-17)^fuel`, `Q = 12008 + 48017 · (2^27 + 2·fuel + 2)` the calls of the honest prover and the verifier together; at most `2^-256` at a fuel of `2^25` (`LeanVM.Rpm.completenessRpm_le`), which the end statement `Completeness` states.

A proof is the Python verifier's format (full Merkle paths); the Rust verifier's expansion of the pruned wire paths is not formalized. That the Lean verifier is the Rust and Python ones is test evidence: it accepts the Rust prover's proofs, gives the Rust verifier's verdict on mutated ones, and its helpers and Fiat-Shamir sponge agree with the Python's on the inputs of the tests.

[`LeanVM.lean`](LeanVM.lean) fails the build if the axioms of any of these theorems grow beyond `propext`, `Classical.choice` and `Quot.sound`.

## How the proofs reach the random-permutation model

This is proof, not statement. Both theorems are first proved with Keccak-256 and Keccak-f[1600] two independent random oracles, `H` on byte strings and `F` a random *function* on states ([`LeanVM/Protocol/Rom/Statement.lean`](LeanVM/Protocol/Rom/Statement.lean): `KnowledgeSoundnessRom`, for the extractor on the adversary's cached Keccak-256 answers, at `soundnessError Q`; `CompletenessRom`, at `completenessError fuel`; proved as `LeanVM.knowledgeSoundnessRom_holds`, [`Protocol/LeanVMSound.lean`](LeanVM/Protocol/LeanVMSound.lean), and `LeanVM.completenessRom_holds`, [`Protocol/LeanVM.lean`](LeanVM/Protocol/LeanVM.lean)). `common`'s indifferentiability of the Keccak-256 sponge from a random oracle, with the duplex's direct permutation calls, moves them to the random-permutation model at a cost of `q^2 / 2^508` for `q` permutation calls (`Common.keccak256_transfer2`).

In [`LeanVM/Protocol/Rpm/`](LeanVM/Protocol/Rpm) and [`LeanVMRpm.lean`](LeanVM/Protocol/LeanVMRpm.lean): the adversary's private coins do not help, its experiment being bounded by that of a deterministic adversary of as many queries to `π` and `π⁻¹` (`Coins.lean`: `rpmExperiment_le_det`, and `rpmRun_le_det` for the batching; at a coin the best of the finitely many continuations, at a query the query and then the best continuation at each answer, the state the permutation's edges and the transcript); the experiment on lane states is `common`'s real world on byte states (`RpmRelabel.lean`, through the bijections of `common`'s `Common/Lanes.lean`, where `keccak256_eq_rpm` also shows the deployed Keccak-256 to be `common`'s sponge over the deployed Keccak-f[1600]), and the Keccak-256 evaluations the extractor reads off a transcript (`transcriptPaths`) are `common`'s `pathsOf` on its bytes (`Paths.lean`, `transcriptPaths_eq`); the verifier and the honest prover follow `common`'s duplex discipline (`Duplex.lean`: the seed's permutation input is zero from lane `8` on, and every later one overwrites lanes `0..15` of an earlier answer and keeps lanes `16..24`, so the capacity and byte `135` are kept, `keccakNF_of_keep`); their permutation calls are counted in `Cost.lean`; the transfer gives the random-oracle adversary `romAdv` (the adversary with the indifferentiability simulator, one oracle query per query of the adversary, with coins), whose Keccak-256 answers are exactly the transcript's evaluations (`Extract.lean`), so `extractorRpm` sees what the random-oracle extractor sees; and an adversary with coins does no better than the best deterministic one (`Coins.lean`), to which `knowledgeSoundnessRom_holds` applies.

## The proof, in phases

The verifier is the composition commit → bus (GKR grand products) → table sumcheck → public input → Keccak reduction (GKR over the rounds of Keccak-f[1600]) → ring switching → WHIR opening of the stack. Its soundness is a round-by-round doom certificate (`LeanVM/Protocol/Compile/`, `EndToEnd/Sound/Assemble.lean`) compiled to the random-oracle model (`Compile/Rom/`), with the leanISA instance (`Protocol/LeanIsa.lean`) carrying the arithmetization (`Arithmetization/`) into the protocol. Two points specific to this leanVM (the `keccak-gkr` design, on `main` since `4af2cdddd`):

* **The `KECCAK` table's lanes.** Its fifty value lanes are not committed. The input lanes are columns of `q_keccak` (slots `0..24`), which also commits the state after the first twelve of the permutation's twenty-four rounds (slots `25..49`) and, in its spare slots `50..63`, the table's first fourteen committed columns (its operands, flags, `free` and `cnt_0`), and the output lanes are *derived*: lane `l` of the last twelve rounds applied to that middle state (`M3Instance.derived`, `LeanIsa.outColumn`). The instance's Keccak clause (`KeccakRegion.Holds`: the middle state is the first twelve rounds of the input) makes the output Keccak-f[1600] of the input (`column_outCol_of_aux`).
* **The Keccak clause through the table sumcheck.** The Keccak reduction checks the middle state only at one point, drawn earlier; so the clause is carried through the table sumcheck as a moving invariant (`Spine/KeccakZero.lean`, `TableSumcheck.inv`), binding the region's coordinates high first and escaping with at most one challenge per round, inside the round's `3/|E|`. The reduction's precondition is then the output claims against the committed middle state and the middle state's agreement at the table point (`Front/KeccakBridge.lean`, `Keccak/` for the GKR proofs).

## The aggregation guest

The previous leanVM (BLAKE2s, Flock) had a formalized aggregation guest (`LeanVM/Guest/`: its compiled bytecode, program logic, a symbolic executor and kernel-checked certificates, `guest_knowledge`, `guest_complete`, `aggregate_sound_outer`, `children_sound`, `batch_rom`). At this leanVM the guest (`crates/rec_aggregation/guests/lean_ethereum.py`) is a different program (it verifies the Keccak-GKR proof system, with ring switching and a duplex transcript); its soundness is re-proved ([the guest's proof](#the-guests-proof)), against the statements of `Statement/LeanVmGuest.lean` and `Statement/LeanVmGuest.lean`, and its completeness within an explicit budget is proved (below), its statement carried also by the files named there. `Protocol/Verifier/Deferred.lean` (the deferring verifier with a true bytecode claim is the deployed one, `verify_of_verifyDeferred`) and `DeferredShape.lean` (`verifyDeferred_shaped`), which the guest's recursion step uses, are ported.

Its data:

* **The data.** leanVM has no `LEANVM_WRITE_*` tests at this pin, so `scripts/guest-export` exports the guest from leanVM's own sources (it compiles `crates/rec_aggregation`'s sources as its own crate, with the exporters as a child module of `aggregation.rs`, reading `../.leanvm` and never writing it): the compiled guest (`Statement/Data/Bytecode*.lean`, the statement's generated data: `2^19` slots, 363,731 instructions in 900 functions, 45,830 of them `KECCAK`), its source AST after the deployed placeholders (`Data/Source.lean`; the exporter checks that it compiles to the same bytecode), the compiler's test programs (`tests/LeanVMTests/Guest/Programs.lean`), the Rust's figures for the guest (`tests/LeanVMTests/Guest/Manifest.lean`) and the node vectors (`tests/LeanVMTests/Spec/NodeVectors.lean`).
* **The bytecode as a `Program`** (`Guest/Code.lean`, `Guest/Bytecode.lean`): instructions in offset form (`OInstr`, `KECCAK` as `.keccak m0 m1 m2 m3 tail b8 cap out inOdd outOdd`, its cells at the offsets `keccakIns`, `keccakOuts`), `guest`, and the fetch lemma at every pc of its functions. That `guest` is the bytecode the Rust proves is checked compiled: its Fiat-Shamir IV, the Keccak-256 hash of its `2^23` bytecode words (`deployedSeed guest`), is the Rust's `fs_seed` (`lake exe node-vectors`).
* **The source AST** (`Guest/Source.lean`, with `[body for v in unroll(lo, hi)]`, `listComp`) and **the core IR** (`Guest/Core.lean`): one `Atom.keccak ins outs inOdd outOdd` per `KECCAK` instruction, its thirteen read and thirteen written cells, meaning `KeccakCells`.

The batching of a node's children, which depends on the statement alone, is re-proved for the Keccak leanVM ([`LeanVM/Guest/Batch/`](LeanVM/Guest/Batch/)). The recursion step `LeanVM.Guest.Batch.children_sound`: for a witness of `NodeRel` under the deployed seed whose statement's deferred claim holds, every child's proof is accepted by the deployed verifier and the child's own deferred claim holds, or a challenge of the node's batching is bad (`BatchBad`: a lambda that the gaps and the earlier lambdas fix, or a sumcheck challenge that is a root of a nonzero quadratic its round fixes; `batch_sound`). That no challenge is bad is the batching's soundness in the random-permutation model, `LeanVM.Guest.Batch.batch_rpm` ([`Guest/Batch/Rpm.lean`](LeanVM/Guest/Batch/Rpm.lean)): an adversary querying `π` and `π⁻¹` outputs a proof, and the batching as a verifier program (`batchAny`, which is `Node.batch` with its deferred claim checked, `runProof_batchAny`), Keccak-256 the sponge over `π` and Keccak-f[1600] `π`, accepts a proof that begins with fewer than `M` children of the bytecode's shapes with a false claim with probability at most `Q · (2^-191 + 5Q/2^256) + (QA + QV · 2^32)^2 / 2^508` for `QA` queries of the adversary, `QV` the program's query bound and `Q = QA + QV`, at most `Q · 2^-188` for `Q ≤ 2^64` (`batch_rpm_bits`). The end statement `BatchSound` ([`Statement/LeanVmGuest.lean`](../Statement/LeanVmGuest.lean), with the program, the event and the query bound) states it for `QA ≤ 2^128` and `QV ≤ 2^96` as `(QA + QV + 1) · 2^-191 + 5 (QA + QV)² · 2^-256`, the transfer term folded into the `+ 1` (`batch_bound_le`), proved by `batch_sound_holds`: the verifier written out (`batchVerifier`: the seed `F(H(label) ‖ 0)`, then `batchAny`). Its proof is the random-oracle bound `batch_rom` (Keccak-256 and Keccak-f[1600] independent random oracles, `Q · (2^-191 + 5Q/2^256)`, `batch_rom_explicit`, `batch_rom_bits`) moved by the transfer of `common` (`LeanVM.Rpm.rpmRun_le_rom`, [`Protocol/Rpm/Generic.lean`](LeanVM/Protocol/Rpm/Generic.lean): the program follows the duplex discipline and makes at most `QV · 2^32` permutation calls). This is the batching run outside the circuit, with Keccak-f[1600] = `π`. It does not compose with the guest's in-circuit statements (`guest_knowledge`, `aggregate_sound_outer`), whose `KECCAK` instruction computes the deployed Keccak-f[1600]: soundness across recursion levels does not follow from it. The guest computes the batching with Keccak itself, so applying it is the Fiat-Shamir assumption for the batching.

### The guest's proof

`LeanVM/Guest/EndFinal.lean` and, for `aggregate_sound_outer` (in the random-permutation model), `LeanVM/Guest/EndRpm.lean`, pinned in [`LeanVM.lean`](LeanVM.lean) (axioms `propext`, `Classical.choice`, `Quot.sound`), for the deployed bytecode `guest` (`2^19` slots, 363,731 instructions, `bytecode = bytecodeWords guest`, `2^23` words) and the decoder `LeanVM.Guest.End.decode`:

```lean
theorem guest_knowledge : GuestKnowledge guest decode
theorem Binding.digest_binds : DigestBinds
theorem aggregate_sound_outer : AggregateSound guest decode
theorem decode_binds {input : PublicInput} {t : Trace guest} (hv : ValidExecution guest input t)
    {iv : Digest} {s₀ : NodeStatement} (hs₀ : s₀.defer.Shaped bytecode)
    (hpub : publicDigest input = statementDigest iv s₀) :
    ((decode t.image).seed = iv ∧ (decode t.image).statement = s₀) ∨
      HasCollision (collisionCandidates (decode t.image).seed (decode t.image).statement iv s₀)
```

* `guest_knowledge`: every valid execution of `guest` on a public input `input` decodes, from its memory, to a seed `d.seed`, a statement `d.statement` with `statementDigest d.seed d.statement = publicDigest input`, and a witness `d.witness` with `NodeRel bytecode d.seed d.statement d.witness` (`Statement/LeanVmGuest.lean`, `DecodesOn`).
* `Binding.digest_binds` (`Statement/LeanVmGuest.lean`, an end statement): two statements whose deferred claims have the shape of one bytecode and whose digests under two seeds are equal are the same statement under the same seed, or one of the four pairs of `collisionCandidates` is a Keccak-256 collision (`Binding.statementDigest_binds`).
* `aggregate_sound_outer` (`AggregateSound`, `Statement/Corollaries.lean`, a corollary: `LeanVM.corollaries_of_theorems` derives it from `KnowledgeSoundness`, `GuestKnowledge` and `DigestBinds`): in the random-permutation model, for an adversary of at most `Q ≤ 2^128` queries to `π` and `π⁻¹`, the probability that the verifier accepts a proof of `guest` on the digest, under the deployed seed `deployedSeed guest`, of a statement `s` whose deferred claim has the guest's shape (`s.defer.Shaped (bytecodeWords guest)`), while `LeanVM.extractorRpm` extracts no execution from the adversary's transcript and the proof, or the extracted one does not decode to `deployedSeed guest`, `s` and a witness of `NodeRel` (or to a seed and statement giving, with `deployedSeed guest` and `s`, a Keccak-256 collision among the four pairs of `collisionCandidates`), is at most `(Q + 2^14) · 2^-128 + 5 (Q + 2^14)² · 2^-256`, the verifier's knowledge-soundness bound.
* `decode_binds`: a consumer whose statement `s₀` has a deferred claim of the bytecode's shape (`DeferredClaim.Shaped bytecode`: `23` coordinates, implied by the outer verifier's check that the claim holds) and, under the seed `iv`, the public input's digest, learns that the decoded seed and statement are `iv` and `s₀`, or holds two different preimages with one Keccak-256 digest among the four pairs (`Binding.statementDigest_binds`).

**Non-vacuity.** The guest's completeness (below) shows that every node within the deployed limits and budget has a valid execution of `guest`, so the theorems above are not vacuous for those nodes. Beyond the budget's coverage, the evidence is tests: `guest-diff` runs the guest's core IR interpreter on four real aggregations of the Rust (a one-signature leaf, a leaf of both schemes with a blob, a leaf whose leanXMSS list is longer than a claim group, a node over them) and checks each witness and stream, and `lake exe node-vectors` ties the bytecode `guest` to the Rust's (`deployedSeed guest` is the Rust's `fs_seed`).

**The decoder** (`EndKnow.decodeWith`, `decodeV`, `EndFinal.decode`) reads the seed and the statement off `main`'s frame (`fp = 1`, the cells of `main`'s certificate: `B1.Main.decSeed`, `decStmt`), and the witness `B1.Main.decWitV σ L.read (Located.frameV L) VerifySub.vsPiOf` off `main`'s valuation `σ`, the memory `L` and the memory's frame map `Located.frameV L`, which gives each call the valuation of its frame, the first frame `a < 2^κ` of the memory at which the callee's code holds on the call's words (`Located.frameOf`). Each leanXMSS and leanSPHINCS signature is read off its check's frame (`Sig.xmssSigOf`, `Sig.sphincsSigOf`), the DA matrix off `da_verify`'s and its callees' frames (`Da.dvWit`), each child's proof off its `verify_sub` frame and the memory (`VerifySub.vsPiOf`: the stream words `verify_sub` reads and the openings its `open_stacked` call's queries hash), the batching's hints off `aggregate_claims`' frame and the memory (`aggHints`). The decoder chooses nothing, computes no hash and runs no verifier of its own; it finds each call's frame as the first of the memory's `2^κ` frames at which the guest's own code for the callee holds on the call's words (a classical test of the guest's semantics, which includes its in-circuit checks), and reads the witness there. `tests/LeanVMTests/Guest/DecodeNoChoice.lean` follows every definition `decode` unfolds to (through `LeanVM`, `LeanXmss`, `Sphincs`, `LeanDa`) and checks that exactly two mention `Classical.choose`, `Classical.epsilon`, `Exists.choose`, `Classical.indefiniteDescription`, `Classical.propDecidable`, `Classical.choice` or `Nat.find`: `Located.frameOf` (`Classical.propDecidable`, the decided test of each frame) and `Semantics.gLog?` (`Classical.choose`, the unique `i` with `a = g^i`, the specification's address); with positive controls (it reaches `Located.frameOf` and `VerifySub.piOfW`, and flags `VerifySub.valOf`, which chooses); and that, stopping at the guest's semantics, none mentions Keccak-256, Keccak-f[1600], a signature scheme's verifier, the deferring verifier, the DA opening or the batching.

**The chain.** `bytecode ⇐ B2 ⇒ core IR ⇐ B1 ⇒ NodeRel`:

* B2 (`Guest/Sound.lean`: `b2_step`, `b2_mainL`; `EndSound.b2SoundL`): checker K's contracts for every compiled function, from the kernel-checked certificates (`Guest/Checked/`, 361 modules), give, for a valid execution, `main`'s core IR body at its frame with every call located at a frame of the memory (`EndLoc.mainBodyL_of`, the run's start `safe_jumpTables`). It takes two facts about the core IR from B1: `exponent_tables` returns the exponent tables (`expTablesSound`, from `exponent_tables_holds`), and `verify_sub`'s first 2098 statements put the 99 committed columns' logs `col_kappas` below 29 (`vsKnown_proved`, from `VerifySub.vsKnown_holds`).
* B1 (`B1/`): `main`'s soundness over any call relation that unfolds along a frame map (`B1.UnfoldsV`), its witness `decWitV` (`B1.Main.main_sound_V_of_sub`), given `verify_sub`'s with its proof `vsPiOf` (`VerifySub.verify_sub_sound_w : Main.SubSoundW bytecode vsPiOf`). The located relation unfolds along the memory's frame map (`Located.unfoldsV_RL`).

## The executable prover

The completeness statements name one honest prover, `LeanVM.prover`: `LeanVM.Prover.prove H F prog input t ρ fuel` of [`Statement/Prover/`](../Statement/Prover/) with the oracle calls `queryH`, `queryF`. It is a program: with Keccak-256 and Keccak-f[1600] in `Id` it runs as `lake exe leanvm-prove`. It computes the witness from the execution (one run of the program, `witness?`; the empty proof unless `t` is a valid execution within `2^28` committed cells, or at an invalid rate), then runs a script of the prover's moves (`Script`) against the verifier's own program `leanVmProgram` (`runScript`): the scalars it sends are absorbed and the challenges squeezed by the Fiat-Shamir sponge, each commitment's Merkle tree is built once and its openings read from it, and each proof of work searches the nonces below `fuel`. The phases are stateful and linear (or `n log n`) in their tables: the stack's codeword by the additive NTT in the novel basis (`Ntt`), the bus's product trees built once and its GKR rounds on folded child tables (`Bus`, `Gkr`), the table sumcheck on per-table folded columns (`Table`), the Keccak GKR on per-layer bit masks with the RMFE products as carry-less multiplications (`Keccak*`), and the WHIR opening on arrays folded in place (`Whir`).

**Why it is the specification's prover** ([`LeanVM/Prover/`](LeanVM/Prover/)). The completeness proof follows the honest strategy `leanVmProver prog s ρ q` (`EndToEnd/Complete/`) at a witness `HonestFor` (or `ChildFor`) chosen classically; `LeanVM/Prover/Complete.lean` restates every completeness theorem for any prover that is `Rom.honestComp` of that strategy at some such witness (`SpecOnValid`, `ChildSpec`). The compiled honest prover `proveFromM` commits each tree once (the query bound is unchanged), and the statement's run of a script is the compiled prover of `Strategy.ofScript` of it, in every monad (`Refine`). Two scripts that make the same moves give the same proof (`Corr`, `runFrom_corr`); each executable phase makes its specification phase's moves (`corr_busFast`, `corr_tableFast`, `corr_pubFast`, `KeccakGkr.corr_keccakPhase`, `corr_ringFast`, `corr_openingFast`, `Enc.stackWordFast_eq`; the sumcheck messages through `coeffsOf_eq`, a coefficient list of the right length that evaluates as the round's claim is the specification's), and the witness is the specification's at `skelWitness prog input t.image t.runSkels` (`witness?_spec`, `childFor_trace`). So `prove queryH queryF` is the specification at the execution's own witness (`prove_eq_honestComp`, `Assemble.lean`), and `LeanVM.completeness_holds`, `guest_child_complete` follow (`Prover/End.lean`).

**Differential test.** `scripts/export-prover-traces-rust.sh <dir>` writes, for leanVM's test programs (the chain of `python_verifier.rs` at several loop counts, `fibonacci.py`; `--corpus` every suite program declaring its public input), the program's bytecode, public input, an execution's memory and size from the Rust interpreter, and the Rust prover's proof and time. `scripts/prover-diff.py <dir>` runs `leanvm-prove` on each, compares its proof with the Rust prover's, and runs `fuzz-verifier.py` on the Lean-made proofs: each must be accepted by the Lean verifier (`leanvm-verify`) and the deployed Rust verifier (`scripts/rust-verifier-cli`), and every mutation rejected by both. CI runs it on `chain4-rate1`:

```sh
scripts/export-prover-traces-rust.sh /tmp/prover-cases
lake build leanvm-prove leanvm-verify
cargo build --release --manifest-path scripts/rust-verifier-cli/Cargo.toml
python3 -I scripts/prover-diff.py /tmp/prover-cases --mutations 60
```

On the corpus (`--corpus`: 17 programs at the four rates, 68 proofs, memory `2^16` to `2^18` words, up to 175,053 instructions) every Lean-made proof is byte-identical to the Rust prover's (as the Rust verifier expands its pruned paths) and accepted by both verifiers, and 748 of 748 mutations of them are rejected by both (12 cases per proof, no verdict difference). The Lean prover takes 34 to 60 s per small proof and 344 to 808 s for `chain5000` (`2^18` words), 5,197 s in all against 290 s for the Rust prover (multithreaded), about 18 times slower; at small sizes most of it is the proofs of work (`2^17` Keccak-f[1600] permutations each, at the statement's compiled Keccak).

## Building and testing

```sh
lake exe cache get
python3 -I ../lake-build.py . LeanVM LeanVMTests

```

While editing proofs, `python3 -I ../lake-build.py . LeanVM --dev` is about 10x faster: it builds into `.lake/build-dev` with the kernel skipped on `LeanVM` (`debug.skipKernelTC`). Ordinary proofs are still checked by elaboration, but the kernel-checked certificates (`decide +kernel`) are not checked at all, so it is never a substitute for the build above.

`lake build` at the repository root runs all of it, with the Rust checks below, for the five projects (`../check-all.sh`).

The guest's kernel-checked certificates (`Guest/Checked`, `Guest/B2C/Shared`, `Guest/B2C/Cplw`, with their data `Guest/Data/{Cert*,Plumb*,Snaps*,Pows,FrameSizes,CplArms,CplParts}`) are written together, from the bytecode and the core IR, by `lake exe guest-cert` (`--check` fails when any is stale). A module holds up to eight checks (windows, parts of windows, groups of arms or subtrees of the powers of `g`, `perFile`): each module costs the kernel about a second to import, and a module of several checks sets `Elab.async false`, without which a module of `B2C` walks keeps the memory of each check to its end. The windows of `verify_sig_lean_sphincs` that are one `match` are checked by groups of eight arms (`Checked/G21_*`, `Shared/H21_*`, `Cplw/D21_*`); what a window's groups compute alike, the executor's run with its index and links and the window's statements (`B2C.winPre`, [`Guest/B2C/SplitPre.lean`](LeanVM/Guest/B2C/SplitPre.lean)), is computed once per window by `kernel_lit` ([`Guest/KernelLit.lean`](LeanVM/Guest/KernelLit.lean), `Checked/X21_*`): the kernel's normal form written into a definition `x21_i`, which the kernel checks equal to `winPre 21 i` (`x21_i_eq`), and from which the groups start (`checkWinS_of_pre`, `cplWinS_of_pre`). The powers of `g` are checked on words in `ℕ` (`PowTree.okWB`, `Guest/Pow.lean`). A module takes 2 to 3.5 GB; an `X21_*` module about 4 GB and the modules of `verify_tables`' windows (`Checked/F11_*`, `F8_0`) about 4.8 GB. `../lake-build.py` admits the modules by their own (anonymous) memory, recorded in `.lake/lake-build.tsv` by each run, over the heavy modules of [`lake-build.tsv`](lake-build.tsv).

The completeness budget's static certificates are generated from the core IR and the certificate data by `python3 -I scripts/count/gen.py . all` (`scripts/count/checker.py` mirrors `Guest/Memory/Static.lean`'s checker): the words at the frame sizes `B2C.fs` (`Guest/Memory/GuestWords`), each opcode's instructions at `B2C.ωArm B2C.xmssArmC op` over the closure of `main`'s statically bounded callees (`Guest/Memory/GuestCnt{X,M,S,D,J,K}`, `GuestCntPm`), of `verify_sub`'s and of the statically bounded callees of `main`'s `claim_list_digest` and `da_verify` (`GuestCntV*`, their tables `Guest/Count/VsCountData.lean`, `MvCountData.lean`) and of `verify_tables`' and `check_opening_terminal`'s (`GuestCntW*`), those callees' bounds (`Guest/Count/Static.lean`) and `verify_bus_gkr`'s and `verify_keccak`'s counts within ratios of their words (`Guest/Count/RatioV*`). `python3 -I scripts/count/gen.py . report` prints the coverage. Their parts take 2 to 3.5 GB each.

The Rust checks run against leanVM at [`../leanvm-rev`](../leanvm-rev), which `../leanvm-tree.sh` checks out into `../.leanvm`, its `formal/lean_vm` a link to this directory. The Rust-vs-Lean differential, as CI runs it:

```sh
../leanvm-tree.sh
lake build leanvm-verify
cargo build --release --manifest-path scripts/rust-verifier-cli/Cargo.toml
python3 -I scripts/fuzz-verifier.py tests/fixtures/verifier --jobs 4
```

After a bump of `../leanvm-rev` to a protocol change, regenerate the fixtures with `scripts/export-verifier-fixtures-rust.sh tests/fixtures/verifier`.

The guest's data (the bytecode, the source, the test programs, the manifest and the node vectors) is regenerated, after a bump of `../leanvm-rev` or a change to the exporter, by one command; `--check`, which CI runs, fails when any of it is stale:

```sh
cargo build --release --manifest-path scripts/guest-export/Cargo.toml
scripts/guest-export/target/release/guest-export .           # write
scripts/guest-export/target/release/guest-export . --check   # CI
```

An independent check of the bytecode data against leanVM's own disassembly: `DBG_DISASM=/tmp/disasm.txt scripts/guest-export/target/release/guest-export . --check`, then `python3 -I scripts/guest-export/roundtrip.py . /tmp/disasm.txt` compares every slot and every function's range.

The guest-diff cases, real aggregations of the Rust (a one-signature leaf, a leaf of both schemes with a blob, one whose leanXMSS list is longer than a claim group, a node over them), each with its statement, seed, witness and installed hint streams, are written by the exporter's ignored tests. The Lean mirror's differential check, `guest-diff` (`cli/GuestDiff.lean`), reads them and checks, per case, that the witness holds the statement (`NodeRel`'s computable parts), that the honest hint generator (`Guest/Hints.lean`, `honestStreams`) gives the Rust's installed streams entry for entry, that the public input is the statement's digest, and that the interpreter runs the guest's core IR on `main` with those streams, consuming all of them:

```sh
LEANVM_GUEST_DIFF=/tmp/guest-diff cargo test --release --manifest-path scripts/guest-export/Cargo.toml --lib guest_diff -- --ignored
lake build guest-diff && .lake/build/bin/guest-diff /tmp/guest-diff/*.txt
```
